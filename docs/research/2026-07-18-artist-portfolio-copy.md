# CIBA portfolio copy research — 2026-07-18

## Question

How should a professor-facing artist portfolio introduce the practice and each project without
promotional language or vague self-evaluation?

## Primary-source findings

- The Royal College of Art asks applicants to edit decisively, stay concise, select work for a
  specific purpose, and state their role clearly. It also asks what values, ideas, methods, and
  techniques underpin the practice.
  Source: <https://www.rca.ac.uk/study/apply-to-study/portfolio-advice/>
- Yale School of Art's submission interface pairs each image with a title, completion date,
  materials, and a brief description. This supports a factual record before interpretation.
  Source: <https://www.art.yale.edu/apply/graduate-admission/portfolio-requirements>
- UAL requires a project description of at least two sentences and says it needs to explain the
  concept behind the work. It separately requires concise alt text for images.
  Source: <https://portfolio.arts.ac.uk/why-has-my-ual-showcase-project-been-rejected/>
- RISD defines an artist statement as a brief text that describes and contextualises work, ideas,
  and intentions. Its prompts begin with what is made, which media and processes are used, and
  which subjects or ideas are addressed; it recommends a natural voice rather than imitating a
  critic or professor.
  Source: <https://artsandlanguage.risd.edu/artist-statements>
- RISD programme guidance also asks for title, materials, dimensions, and date, and for the maker's
  role to be identified where collaboration or technical assistance is involved.
  Source: <https://www.risd.edu/academics/illustration/masters-program>

## CIBA copy rule derived for this site

Every public project starts with this record:

1. Title
2. Year or date range
3. Medium / tools
4. Current status
5. Concept: one or two plain sentences naming what the work does and what question it examines
6. Evidence: images, build, archive, or repository that actually exists
7. Role / collaborators when applicable

Use direct nouns and verbs. Distinguish a finished work from work in development. Do not say the
site is beautiful, immersive, curator-readable, easy to understand, or successful; those are
judgements for the reader. Do not invent narrative, exhibition history, scale, collaborators, or
technical facts that the artist has not confirmed.

## Copy changes applied

- The practice introduction now states that CIBA currently makes games and web-based works in
  Tokyo and names the four public projects.
- The second paragraph states the four project questions in parallel, without evaluating them.
- Works windows expose Year / Medium / Status / Concept before the link to the complete record.
- The home introduction states the current inventory: two games in development and two web works
  from 2023.
- Chinese, English, and Japanese use the same factual hierarchy rather than three independently
  embellished versions.
